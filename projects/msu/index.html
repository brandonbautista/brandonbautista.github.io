The role

A broad brief, made workable

After 25 years at Michigan Virtual, including a decade as Director of Marketing, I wanted to spend more time making the work myself. At MSU, I lead communications for an academic department and handle the day-to-day writing, production, and publishing that comes with it.

I work across research and people stories, a monthly email, social channels, the department website, events, and physical communications. The range makes an editorial system useful: a clear intake process, an interview guide, review steps, and a way to adapt each story for the channel where someone will find it.

Featured work · 01

A monthly newsletter with a familiar voice

The department had an existing student spotlight, Field Voices, created by academic advisor Nico Gisholt and my predecessor, Louise Henderson. I proposed making it the lead feature of a broader monthly newsletter, alongside department stories, courses, and events. Nico continues to supply student submissions; the department chair reviews each issue.

I set the structure, directed the template and production workflow, and manage the publication in Campaign Monitor. I directed the template and reviewed it for the email clients it needed to serve.

The first send recorded a 39.84% open rate against the 28.5% education benchmark available at the time. It is one issue, so I treat that as a promising start, not a trend.

Featured work · 02

Stories shaped with the people behind them

I choose story angles, interview sources, edit, coordinate faculty fact checks, make supporting graphics, and adapt copy for each social channel. My default is an email interview: it gives sources room to answer carefully and creates a record I can check before publishing.

In anthropology, some subjects need specialist review and informed consent. My job is to route the work to the right people and respect their expertise. I do not treat that review as a communications formality.

The work is collaborative. Sources provide the substance, faculty review technical details, and I’m responsible for the editorial direction, editing, and final review. I do not claim sole authorship of individual stories here.

Featured work · 03

A website that helps people find their way

I administer the department’s WordPress sites and proposed a clearer hierarchy around audiences and anthropology subfields. A prototype is under review; the proposed structure is not live. Faculty supply academic content, while the college web team manages the theme and plugins.

I also handle day-to-day web stewardship and accessibility work. That includes improving image descriptions and coordinating work on older sites. There is no finished accessibility result to report yet.
